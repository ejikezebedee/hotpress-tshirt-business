# Hot-Press T-Shirt Business — Business Plan

**Audience:** Internal operating plan + lender/funding readiness (equity not required at this scale).
**Architecture:** Advanced Business Plan Architecture (11-section, Sequoia × YC × a16z unified).
**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Currency:** EUR (research sources in USD; converted at ~0.92 EUR/USD and rounded — see Assumptions Ledger).

---

## 1. Company purpose

A capital-light custom-apparel studio that produces high-quality, on-demand printed T-shirts and branded merch for small businesses, teams, clubs, events, and individuals — using a heat press and outsourced DTF (direct-to-film) transfers, so customers get professional full-color prints without minimum-order barriers.

---

## 2. Problem

Three unmet needs in the mid-market custom-apparel space:

1. **Small buyers are locked out.** Traditional screen printing demands 25–50+ minimums and high setup fees, so a 5-person team, a birthday event, or a solo creator can't get affordable full-color apparel.
2. **Quality/consistency risk.** Cheap print-on-demand marketplaces produce variable print quality, slow shipping, and no local face-to-face accountability.
3. **Speed and local trust.** Local buyers (clubs, events, small businesses in the region) want a reliable local producer with fast turnaround and one accountable contact — not a faceless overseas drop-shipper.

**Evidence of pain:** print-on-demand remains the fastest-growing channel in the category, yet buyer complaints cluster on quality consistency and turnaround — exactly the gap a local, heat-press operator with outsourced professional DTF closes.

---

## 3. Solution

A heat-press studio that:
- **Owns the heat press** (low-cost, high-quality press) but **outsources DTF transfer printing** to professional transfer suppliers — eliminating the €2,000+ printer, ink, powder, and maintenance burden while keeping full-color, photo-quality output.
- **Produces on demand** with no minimums, 2–5 day local turnaround.
- **Serves both** direct local buyers (walk-in/collection, teams, events) and online buyers (website + marketplace).
- **Adds a design-lite service:** converts rough ideas/logos into press-ready art.

The mechanism: DTF transfers are the "component" — the studio's value-add is selection, design, pressing quality, and local accountability, not owning a printer.

---

## 4. Why now

- **DTF went mainstream.** DTF has moved from "emerging technology" to the standard production method for small-order custom apparel, making pro-grade full-color transfers cheap and universally available (€0.50–3.00 per transfer).
- **Print-on-demand + e-commerce growth.** Global custom t-shirt printing market is growing at high single-digit to low double-digit CAGR, driven by personalization demand and digital printing adoption.
- **Counterfactual:** five years ago, high-quality full-color small-batch printing required a DTG machine or screen setup (€3k–15k) — now it's a €600 heat press plus outsourced transfers.

## 6. Competition & moat

| Competitor type | Examples | Their weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Print-on-demand platforms | Printful, Printify, Redbubble | No local presence, generic quality, slow |
| Local screen-print shops | regional printers | High minimums, slow for small jobs |
| DIY (buyer's own iron-on) | — | Poor durability and quality |
| Other DTF/heat-press sellers | Etsy/Instagram sellers | Race to the bottom on price, no brand |

**Moat (honest assessment):** this is a low-barrier business, so durable advantage must be *earned*, not claimed. The defensible moats are:
1. **Local relationships + speed** (switching cost from a trusted local supplier).
2. **Service depth** (design-lite, guided ordering) that price-only sellers skip.
3. **Repeat B2B accounts** (teams, businesses reordering each season).

Weakness to manage: no proprietary tech; the barrier is operational excellence and customer retention, not IP.

## 7. Product / service

**Current state (launch):**
- Heat press (16"×20" commercial-grade) — owned.
- Outsourced DTF transfers from 1–2 vetted suppliers.
- Blank apparel: mid-weight 100% cotton + poly-blend tees, plus hoodies/sweats as upsell.
- Design-lite: logo cleanup and press-ready art prep.

**Roadmap:**
1. Launch: tees only, local + online.
2. +Hoodies, sweats, tote bags (same heat press, no new capex).
3. +Team/uniform B2B accounts (volume discount tier).
4. (Optional, scale gate) In-house DTF printer once volume justifies ~€2–4k capex to raise margin from ~€2.2 to ~€0.8 per transfer.

**Key risks:** transfer-supplier quality variance (mitigate: 2 suppliers + wash tests); blank-stock inventory (mitigate: just-in-time reorder, low initial stock).

---

## 8. Business model

**Archetype:** transactional / made-to-order (plus a subscription-like repeat layer from B2B reorders).

- **Who pays:** individuals, teams/clubs, events, small businesses.
- **How much / how often:** €20–25 per tee; one-off orders plus seasonal B2B repeats.
- **Revenue shape:** transactional, no recurring base — growth depends on repeat accounts and marketing.
- **One metric that matters:** **repeat-order rate** (share of revenue from returning accounts), targeting 40%+ by year 2.

---

## 9. Unit economics

Per shirt (EUR):

| Line | € |
|---|---|
| Blank garment | 3.50 |
| DTF transfer (outsourced, A4) | 2.20 |
| Labor/pressing (~8 min) | 2.00 |
| Packaging/labels | 0.60 |
| **Total variable cost** | **8.30** |
| Marketplace/processing fees (blended) | 2.20 |
| **Full cost per shirt** | **10.50** |
| Retail price | **22.00** |
| **Contribution margin** | **11.50 (52%)** |

**CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ~€8–12 per new customer at launch (paid social + local); organic/local reduces this toward €3–5.
**LTV (lifetime value):** a repeat B2B account worth €600+ gross over a season; a retail customer ~€40–60. LTV/CAC ≥ 3× is achievable once repeat rate exceeds ~35%.
**Payback on acquisition:** 1–2 orders.

**Sensitivity:**
- If transfer cost rises +30% (€2.20→€2.86): contribution falls to €10.84 (−6%).
- If retail price −20% (€22→€17.60): contribution falls to €7.10 (−38%) → signals a hard price floor around €18–19.

---

## 10. Team

- **Founder (solo):** owns design, pressing, customer contact, and order management. Founder's capability de-risks the technical pressing quality and local selling.
- **Most important missing hire:** a part-time production/design assistant once volume exceeds ~150 shirts/month — the first scaling bottleneck.

## 11. Financials & ask

**Startup capital (capex + initial working capital):**

| Item | € |
|---|---|
| Heat press (commercial 16"×20") | 600 |
| Initial blank stock + samples | 250 |
| Design software (annual) | 100 |
| Website + domain (first year) | 150 |
| Marketing seed | 150 |
| Transfer samples + packaging | 100 |
| **Total** | **≈ 1,350 (round to €1,500 with buffer)** |

**3-year projection (contribution method):**

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shirts sold | 1,100 | 2,400 | 3,600 |
| Revenue (@€22) | €24,200 | €52,800 | €79,200 |
| Variable cost (@€8.30) | €9,130 | €19,920 | €29,880 |
| Fees (@€2.20) | €2,420 | €5,280 | €7,920 |
| **Contribution (@€11.50)** | **€12,650** | **€27,600** | **€41,400** |
| Fixed costs (€320/mo) | €3,840 | €3,840 | €3,840 |
| **Operating result** | **€8,810** | **€23,760** | **€37,560** |

**Breakeven:** €3,840 / €11.50 ≈ **334 shirts/year ≈ 28 shirts/month.**

**Funding ask:** ~€1,500 working capital. Self-fundable; if external, a micro-grant or small working-capital facility, not equity. **Use of funds:** heat press, initial stock, website, marketing seed. **Milestone:** first 100 orders and a repeat-order rate ≥ 30% within 6 months.
